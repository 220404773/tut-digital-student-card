Cookie Policy

What this system stores in your browser, and why

Last updated: 28 August 2026

In short: this system uses only the cookies it needs in order to work. There are no advertising cookies, no analytics cookies, and no third-party tracking cookies.

1. What cookies are

A cookie is a small text file that a website asks your browser to store and send back on later requests. Cookies that are essential for a site to function — for example, to remember that you are signed in — are generally referred to as "strictly necessary".

2. The cookies this system uses

Cookies set by this system, their purpose and lifetime
Name Type Purpose Lifetime
sessionid Strictly necessary Keeps you signed in as you move between pages. Without it you would be signed out on every click. 20 minutes from your last activity, then it expires and you are signed out
csrftoken Strictly necessary (security) Protects forms against cross-site request forgery, an attack in which another site tries to submit a form as you. Up to 1 year
cookie_notice_ack Strictly necessary Remembers that you have seen the cookie notice, so it is not shown to you on every page. 1 year

In live use, sessionid and csrftoken are marked Secure, so your browser only ever sends them over an encrypted HTTPS connection.

3. Other browser storage

One item is stored using your browser's local storage rather than a cookie:

  • pwaInstallDismissedAt — if you dismiss the "Install TUT Card" prompt, this records when you did so, so that you are not asked again for 14 days. It stays in your browser, is never sent to the server, and contains no personal information.

4. Cookies this system does not use

This system does not use analytics cookies, advertising or targeting cookies, social media cookies, or any third-party tracking cookies. There are no embedded videos, social media widgets, comment systems, chat widgets, advertising tags or tracking pixels anywhere on the site.

5. Third-party requests

Every page loads its styling and icon files from two public content delivery networks: jsDelivr (Bootstrap) and Cloudflare cdnjs (Font Awesome). These requests do not set cookies for this site, but as with any request your browser makes, those providers can see your IP address, your browser user-agent and the page that requested the file. This is described in section 5 of the Privacy Policy.

6. Server-side activity records

Separately from cookies, the system keeps its own records of page visits and card activity, including IP addresses. These are described in sections 3 and 7 of the Privacy Policy. They are not controlled by your cookie settings.

7. Managing cookies

Every browser lets you view, block and delete cookies through its settings. Because the cookies listed in section 2 are strictly necessary, blocking or deleting them will sign you out and will prevent forms — including sign-in — from being submitted successfully. There are no optional cookies to switch off, which is why this site shows a short notice rather than a consent chooser.
